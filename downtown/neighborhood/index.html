№ 01 — The Quarter

Downtown
is the amenity.

Our flats sit inside Downtown's early-twentieth-century European rows — high ceilings, ironwork balconies, parquet underfoot — in the part of Cairo that still walks. The Egyptian Museum is a ten-minute stroll, Tahrir Square twelve, the Nile at Qasr el-Nil twenty. Cafés on the corner, the metro under the kerb, and the whole city on tap.

This guide is how we'd show you around on day one — everything measured from the front door, at an honest pace.

№ 02 — Field Notes

The day, in three lights.

How the quarter runs from six in the morning to midnight — the short version we give every guest.

02 / 08

Morning

Bourgeois balconies

The buildings here are from the 1920s — high ceilings, ironwork, plaster mouldings, and flats that still carry their original parquet. Qasr El Nil and Talaat Harb are a short walk in any direction; the metro is closer than the kerb. Look up on your way out.

Afternoon

Tahrir and around

The Egyptian Museum is minutes on foot — go before the coaches. So is Talaat Harb Square, and the best koshari in the city sits on a corner you'd walk past without noticing. Ask us; we keep the list.

Evening

The Downtown sound

This is the loudest, most alive part of Cairo after dark — traffic, conversation, a wedding, the call to prayer rolling across the rooftops. Close the double-glazed windows and it all goes soft.
